RPS POKER - CANDIDATE REPORT

STATUS

Two live candidate versions remain:

1. Nine-stack Poker: real chips, free betting, escalating antes.
2. First-to-five Poker: permanent match pips, fixed betting.

Both use the same single-community-move hand-ranking system. They differ in
what a bet risks, how the match progresses, and how match length is controlled.


SHARED CORE

NORMAL POKER HAND

1. Both players secretly lock Rock, Paper, or Scissors.
2. One random community move is revealed publicly.
3. Each player's locked move resolves against the community move.
4. The result becomes that player's private hand strength.
5. Players bet without knowing the opponent's locked move.
6. Called hands and check-check hands reach showdown.
7. Folded locked moves remain secret.

SHOWDOWN RANKING

- Win against community = High.
- Tie against community = Middle.
- Loss against community = Low.
- High beats Middle.
- Middle beats Low.
- Identical results tie.

Example community move: Rock.

Locked move    Result against Rock    Strength
Paper          Win                    High
Rock           Tie                    Middle
Scissors       Loss                   Low

The community move must appear after players lock. Choosing after community
reveal would let both players select High every hand.

Every move has an equal chance to become High, Middle, or Low before community
reveal. Normal RPS habits still matter probabilistically. If an opponent always
locks Rock, Paper outranks it on community Rock and community Paper, while Rock
outranks Paper on community Scissors.

The original two-move community pair was discarded. It created strong, weak,
and volatile hands, but required another random reveal after betting. The
single community move removes post-bet randomness and gives each locked move a
known rank before betting.


CANDIDATE A: NINE-STACK POKER

IDENTITY

Full commitment to actual wagering. Players own chips, put those chips at risk,
and may lose previously accumulated progress. Unrestricted sizing allows small
value bets, large bluffs, raises, and all-ins.

MATCH GOAL

- Each player begins with 9 chips.
- There are 18 chips total.
- There are no separately scored rounds.
- A player busts at 0 chips.
- The player possessing all 18 chips wins the match.

ESCALATING ANTE

- Hand 1 ante: 1 chip each.
- Hand 2 ante: 2 chips each.
- Hand 3 ante: 3 chips each.
- The scheduled ante increases by 1 every hand, including after a tied hand.
- If both players can pay the full scheduled ante, both do so and play a normal
  Poker hand.
- If either player cannot pay the full scheduled ante, the shorter stack posts
  all remaining chips and the opponent matches that amount.

NORMAL-HAND BETTING

- One player acts first.
- First actor is random on Hand 1, then alternates every hand.
- Actions: check, bet, call, raise, fold.
- Bets use whole chips.
- Minimum opening bet: 1 chip.
- A normal raise must increase the wager by at least the previous betting
  increment.
- A player may always move all-in even when the remaining amount is smaller
  than a normal minimum raise.
- No player can wager more than the opponent can match.
- Unmatched excess never enters the pot.
- Betting ends on call, fold, or all-in call.

NORMAL-HAND RESOLUTION

- Fold: opponent takes pot; locked moves remain secret.
- Call or check-check: reveal locked moves and compare community results.
- Better result takes entire pot.
- Tied result splits pot evenly.
- Because all contributions are matched, a split restores exact pre-hand
  stacks.
- Scheduled ante still increases after a tied hand.

FORCED ALL-IN ANTE

Posting an ante can consume the shorter player's entire stack. Betting then has
no function. Do not use the community-ranking sequence in this state.

- Skip community reveal.
- Skip betting.
- Play standard Rock Paper Scissors directly for the pot.
- A decisive throw awards the entire pot.
- A tied throw keeps pot in place and repeats standard RPS with fresh moves.
- If the short player wins, chips redistribute and the match may return to
  normal Poker on the next hand.
- If the large stack wins, opponent reaches 0 and match ends.

This direct-RPS ending was adopted after testing showed that locking moves,
revealing community, and comparing ranks without any betting was merely RPS
with unnecessary random steps.

PLAYTEST

Starting stacks: 9-9.

Hand 1, ante 1:

- Pot began at 2; stacks behind were 8-8.
- CHATMAN bet 2 and JP called.
- Community Scissors.
- CHATMAN Rock/High beat JP Scissors/Middle.
- Stacks became CHATMAN 12-6 JP.

Hand 2, ante 2:

- Pot began at 4; stacks behind were CHATMAN 10, JP 4.
- JP bet all 4 chips.
- CHATMAN folded Low.
- Fold transferred only the ante; stacks became CHATMAN 10-8 JP.

Hand 3, ante 3:

- Pot began at 6; stacks behind were CHATMAN 7, JP 5.
- CHATMAN bet 2 and JP called.
- Both revealed Paper/Middle against community Paper.
- Pot split; stacks restored to CHATMAN 10-8 JP.
- Ante advanced despite tie.

Hand 4, ante 4:

- Pot began at 8; stacks behind were CHATMAN 6, JP 4.
- JP checked.
- CHATMAN bet 2 with Low.
- JP raised all-in to 4 total.
- CHATMAN folded.
- Stacks swung to JP 14-4 CHATMAN.

Terminal experiment:

- The next scheduled ante exceeded CHATMAN's stack.
- A first attempt used community ranking with no betting. It immediately felt
  like RPS with extra random steps and was rejected.
- Direct standard RPS was substituted for forced ante all-ins.
- JP Rock beat CHATMAN Scissors and JP finished with all 18 chips.

NINE-STACK FINDINGS

- Match lasted roughly five hands despite beginning with 18 chips.
- Escalating antes successfully prevented a long one-chip random walk.
- Actual owned-chip risk made folds, calls, raises, and bluffs consequential.
- Variable sizing mattered. A 2-chip bet, 4-chip shove, and all-in raise created
  different pressure.
- A fold after a large bet lost only committed chips, allowing dramatic stack
  reversals without automatically ending match.
- Advancing ante after a tied split prevented ties from freezing match pace.
- Forced standard RPS creates a clear all-in climax and removes poker only when
  betting has become impossible anyway.
- Stack totals, pot size, scheduled ante, amount behind, matched-wager limits,
  raise minimums, and position must all be communicated.
- Match length is pressured but not strictly bounded because direct-RPS ties
  can repeat and a short stack can win an all-in ante.
- Escalating ante can dominate strategy quickly. Hand 4 already began with 8
  of 18 chips committed before either player acted.
- The candidate is the most authentic, dramatic, and mechanically heavy form.

NINE-STACK OPEN QUESTIONS

- Does ante increasing every hand feel thrilling or rushed?
- Should the ante rise after tied normal hands?
- Are raises worth their interface and rules cost?
- Is the normal minimum-raise rule understandable enough for this game?
- Does a forced standard-RPS all-in feel like a satisfying climax or like the
  Poker game giving up?
- Should first actor alternate through forced-RPS hands even though position
  has no effect there?
- Is 9 the correct starting stack for an ante schedule beginning at 1?
- How often does a short player survive forced all-in ante and return to Poker?


CANDIDATE B: FIRST-TO-FIVE POKER

IDENTITY

Full commitment to the existing permanent-score language. Players never risk
points already won. A bet offers to double the value of the current hand.
Bluffing, calling, folding, and hidden strength remain, but literal chip
wagering does not.

The score should use the same pips or round markers as other variants. "Score
chips" was temporary development language and is not required player-facing
terminology.

MATCH GOAL

- Both players begin at 0.
- First player to 5 pips wins.
- Won pips are permanent.
- A 2-pip award may take a player past 5.

FIXED BETTING

- One player acts first.
- First actor is random on Hand 1, then alternates every hand.
- First actor may check or bet.
- After a check, second actor may check or bet.
- After a bet, opponent may call or fold.
- There are no raises or variable sizes.

SCORING

- Check-check, decisive showdown: winner gains 1 pip.
- Bet-fold: bettor gains 1 pip; no reveal.
- Bet-call, decisive showdown: winner gains 2 pips.
- Any tied showdown: both players gain 1 pip.

A bet therefore proposes double stakes:

- Folding concedes the normal 1-pip hand.
- Calling accepts a 2-pip showdown.
- A successful bluff gains 1 pip.
- A called value bet can gain 2 pips.
- Already-earned pips never enter a pot.

SIMULTANEOUS MATCH POINT

- If a tie makes both players reach 5 together, neither wins yet.
- Continue sudden-death Poker hands.
- A fold or decisive showdown wins match.
- Further ties repeat without producing a winner.

MATCH LENGTH

- Every hand distributes at least 1 total pip.
- Match reaches a winner or simultaneous match point within 9 hands.
- A player can win in 3 hands through three called 2-pip wins.
- Sudden-death ties can extend the match beyond 9 hands.

PLAYTEST

Result: CHATMAN defeated JP 6-1 after 5 hands.

- Every hand contained a bet.
- Three bets ended in folds and awarded 1 pip.
- One called bet tied and awarded 1 pip to each player.
- One called bet was decisive and awarded 2 pips to winner.
- No folded move was revealed during play.
- Score advanced every hand.
- Match required 10 locked-move decisions and 11 betting actions total.
- With Low facing a bet, caller could not win. Best possible result was tie, so
  folding was correct.
- At 1-4, trailing JP bet because passive loss would also end match, while a
  2-pip win offered comeback progress.

FIRST-TO-FIVE FINDINGS

- Match length was tight and comparable to classic RPS first to five.
- Existing scoreboard can communicate all persistent state.
- No antes, owned-chip stacks, pots, raise rules, matched-stack limits, round
  resets, busted thresholds, or split-pot accounting are required.
- Permanent pips solve the original back-and-forth length problem.
- Ties advance score instead of replaying.
- Betting retains informational pressure but is not literal wagering.
- Calling with Low cannot produce a relative win. This creates some automatic
  folds.
- Every tested hand contained a bet. Betting may be strategically dominant or
  merely common under this tiny sample.
- The candidate is fastest, clearest, least poker-authentic, and easiest to fit
  into the nine-game lineup.

FIRST-TO-FIVE OPEN QUESTIONS

- Is betting frequent because it is interesting, or because checking is weak?
- Do fixed stakes provide enough betting texture?
- Do players accept "bet" when already-won pips are never at risk?
- Should tied check-check and bet-call showdowns both award 1 each?
- Does first to five feel consistent with classic RPS?
- Is sudden death after simultaneous match point clear?
- Does Low having no winning call outcome make too many decisions automatic?


DISCARDED INTERMEDIATE: THREE-CHIP ROUNDS

An intermediate candidate used real chips with the following structure:

- 3 chips each.
- Ante 1.
- Fixed bet 1.
- A round ended when one player reached 5 of the 6 chips.
- Chips reset to 3-3.
- Match was first to 2 round wins.
- Tied pots split.

Why it was attempted:

- A called bet could end a round after one hand.
- Permanent round wins prevented whole-match coin reversals.
- Fixed betting removed shallow choice between betting 1 and shoving 2.

What testing found:

- The official first-to-2 match ended 2-0 after only 3 hands.
- An extra stress-test round lasted 9 hands.
- Four ties occurred in that extra round.
- Stacks repeatedly reversed between 4-2 and 2-4.
- At 4-2, position strongly constrained decisions.
- A leading first actor's bet forced trailing player to call because folding
  also conceded round at 5-1.
- A trailing bettor could let leader fold and restore 3-3.
- The 5-of-6 busted threshold prevented no-betting hands but felt patched.
- System required both chip economy and permanent round scoreboard.

Verdict: discard. It combined complexity of both live candidates without fully
delivering either one's identity.


OTHER DISCARDED RULES

COMMUNITY PAIR

Two public possible moves originally created Strong, Weak, and Volatile hands.
One of those moves was randomly selected after betting. It produced poker hand
strength but added arbitrary post-decision randomness. Replaced by one community
move revealed before betting.

CARRIED TIED POTS

Tied all-ins carried pot into fresh hands. With no chips outside pot, later
hands had no betting and became community-ranked RPS with extra steps. Replaced
by split pots in normal real-chip Poker and productive ties in pip Poker.

NO ANTE

Removing ante left check-check hands with no pot and no progress. Any repair
effectively recreated a forced stake. Rejected for real-chip Poker.

THREE-CHIP VARIABLE BETTING

Bet 1 and shove 2 looked different but often produced same final round result.
Shove created extra fold pressure without meaningfully greater consequence.
Replaced by fixed betting in small-stack experiments.


DIRECT COMPARISON

Nine-stack Poker:

- Real owned chips: yes.
- Literal wagering: yes.
- Bet sizing: unrestricted.
- Raises: yes.
- Persistent progress: reversible.
- Length control: escalating ante.
- Normal-hand ties: split pot.
- Terminal pressure: direct standard-RPS all-in.
- Presentation load: high.
- Drama and comeback potential: high.
- Poker authenticity: high.

First-to-five Poker:

- Real owned chips: no; uses permanent match pips.
- Literal wagering: no; bet doubles current hand value.
- Bet sizing: fixed.
- Raises: no.
- Persistent progress: permanent.
- Length control: first to 5; every hand scores.
- Ties: 1 pip each.
- Terminal pressure: simultaneous-match-point sudden death.
- Presentation load: low.
- Drama and comeback potential: moderate.
- Poker authenticity: moderate.


DECISION

No winner selected yet.

Nine-stack Poker is the bolder star candidate if escalating antes and the
forced direct-RPS ending feel good across more tests. First-to-five Poker is the
safer lineup candidate if permanent scoring still produces meaningful checks,
calls, and bluffs under experienced play.

Next useful tests:

1. Play Nine-stack Poker again with deliberate small bets and cautious checks.
2. Test a forced-ante all-in where short stack wins and Poker resumes.
3. Play First-to-five Poker with both players intentionally checking some
   Middle and Low hands.
4. Track first-actor results in both candidates.
5. Compare total decisions, elapsed time, folds, calls, and ties over at least
   five matches per candidate.
