RPS POKER - VARIANT CANDIDATE

ALTERNATE NAME

Heads-Up RPS

"Heads-up" means a two-player poker game. This name keeps the poker connection
while allowing the presentation to remain system-focused rather than using
literal playing-card or casino imagery. Possible drawback: players unfamiliar
with poker terminology may not realize that betting is the defining mechanic.

STATUS

Promising candidate for the nine-game lineup.

The original locked-RPS betting concept failed because every RPS move has equal
intrinsic strength. Bets could only express habits or second thoughts, so calls
were usually automatic. The community-pair system fixes this. A public random
pair makes each locked move strong, weak, or volatile before betting begins.

The locked RPS move functions like a poker hole card. The community pair
functions like a public board. No private modifier deck or card knowledge is
required; the entire system comes from the normal RPS triangle.


MATCH GOAL

- Two players begin with 5 coins each.
- A player wins the match by possessing all 10 coins.
- Coins are fictional game resources, not real-money wagers.


HAND SEQUENCE

1. ANTE

- Each player puts 1 coin into the pot.
- An ante is paid only when starting a new pot.
- If a showdown ties, the existing pot carries into a fresh hand without
  another ante.

2. LOCK MOVES

- Each player secretly locks Rock, Paper, or Scissors.
- Locked moves cannot change during the hand.

3. DEAL THE COMMUNITY PAIR

One of the three different-move pairs is selected uniformly at random and
revealed publicly:

- Rock + Paper
- Paper + Scissors
- Scissors + Rock

4. BETTING ROUND

- One player acts first.
- The first bettor is chosen randomly for the first hand and alternates every
  hand afterward, including hands played for a carried pot.
- Available actions are check, bet, call, raise, and fold.
- Bets and raises use whole coins.
- The minimum bet or raise is 1 coin.
- There is no fixed betting limit, but no player can risk more than the opponent
  can match. Unmatched excess is never placed into the pot.
- Raises may continue until a player calls, folds, or becomes all-in.
- If both players check, proceed directly to the community reveal.
- If a player folds, the opponent takes the pot. Both locked moves remain
  secret.

5. COMMUNITY REVEAL

- If betting reaches a call or check-check, one of the two community moves is
  selected at random with equal 50% probability.
- Both players reveal their locked moves.
- Each locked move resolves independently against the selected community move
  using normal RPS rules.

6. COMPARE RESULTS

Results are ranked:

- Win beats Tie.
- Tie beats Loss.
- Better result takes the entire pot.
- Equal results carry the entire pot into a fresh hand.

Against one community move, Rock, Paper, and Scissors produce exactly one Win,
one Tie, and one Loss. Therefore equal results occur when both players locked
the same move.


HAND STRENGTH

Example community pair: Paper + Rock.

Locked move    If Paper reveals    If Rock reveals    Hand type
Paper          Tie                 Win                Strong
Scissors       Win                 Loss               Volatile
Rock           Loss                Tie                Weak

- Strong hand: can Win or Tie against the community.
- Weak hand: can Lose or Tie against the community.
- Volatile hand: can Win or Lose against the community.

Every move is fair before the community pair appears:

- One possible pair makes it strong.
- One possible pair makes it weak.
- One possible pair makes it volatile.

"Strong" describes safety against the community. It does not guarantee victory
against every opponent move.

On the Paper + Rock board:

- Paper beats Rock at showdown regardless of the community reveal.
- Paper versus Scissors is 50/50.
- Rock versus Scissors is 50/50.


WHY BETTING WORKS

The community pair gives players real private hand strength to bet around.

- Strong hands can make value bets.
- Weak hands can fold or bluff.
- Volatile hands can bluff-catch or gamble.
- The opponent sees the public pair but does not know the locked move.
- Folded moves remain hidden, so bluffs are not automatically verified.

This creates actual poker situations without a separate deck of secret powers.


RPS HABITS STILL MATTER

The original RPS counter cycle survives probabilistically.

If an opponent always locks Rock, always locking Paper is the best response:

- If the final community move is Rock, Paper scores Win while Rock scores Tie.
- If the final community move is Paper, Paper scores Tie while Rock scores Loss.
- If the final community move is Scissors, Paper scores Loss while Rock scores
  Win.

Across all fair community deals and reveals, Paper beats Rock in 2/3 of
showdowns. Rock escapes in the remaining 1/3. Habit exploitation is valuable
without determining every showdown automatically.


TIES AND ALL-INS

- A tied showdown carries the pot.
- Players lock fresh moves.
- A fresh community pair is dealt.
- No additional ante is paid while the pot is carried.
- Betting position continues to alternate.
- A player with no coins outside the pot cannot bet or call.
- If betting is impossible because a player is all-in, proceed directly through
  fresh locked moves, community pair, community reveal, and showdown.
- If both players are all-in and tie, repeat free hands until somebody wins the
  pot.


IMPARTIAL DEALER

Prototype script:

scripts/rps-poker-dealer.js

Deal a uniformly random community pair:

node scripts/rps-poker-dealer.js deal

Randomly select one move from a dealt pair:

node scripts/rps-poker-dealer.js reveal ROCK PAPER

The script uses Node's cryptographic random-number generator and rejects invalid
or duplicate reveal pairs.


FAKE PLAYTEST FINDINGS

The first test used locked RPS with betting but no community pair.

- Calls were driven mainly by pot odds and stack survival.
- No move had credible strength.
- Every bet was effectively a bluff.
- Small stacks repeatedly removed the betting phase through all-ins.
- The concept felt like RPS with wagering, not poker.

The community-pair test used 5 coins each, ante 1, unrestricted bet sizes, and
carried pots.

It produced three recognizable poker situations:

- A weak hand folded to pressure without revealing.
- A weak hand bluffed and was called by a volatile hand.
- A weak hand shoved and was called by a strong hand that had it dominated.

The second test ended 10-0 after three pots. The central betting mechanism felt
valid, but the economy was extremely fast and snowbally.


OPEN QUESTIONS

- Are 5 starting coins enough for a satisfying match?
- Do unrestricted raises add useful decisions or just produce tedious one-coin
  raising?
- How large is the first-bettor advantage or disadvantage in short matches?
- Does folding occur often enough under strong play?
- Does the final random community reveal feel dramatic or arbitrary?
- Should equal showdown results always carry the pot, or should any cases split
  it?
- Does alternating betting position every carried hand feel fair and readable?
- What player-facing names should replace technical terms such as community
  pair, community reveal, strong, weak, and volatile?
- What betting controls make bet sizing fast on mouse, controller, and touch?


CURRENT VERDICT

The community-pair system is the core of RPS Poker and should be preserved.
It turns a locked RPS move into meaningful private information using only the
existing RPS relationship. The main remaining work is match economy, betting
flow, interface, and balance rather than finding a reason for betting to exist.
