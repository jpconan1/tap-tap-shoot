RPS MINUS ONE - QUICK REPORT

RULES

1. Each player reveals two different RPS moves.
2. Each player simultaneously chooses one revealed move to keep.
3. The kept moves resolve as normal rock-paper-scissors.

RESULT

The complete game is fair. Under optimal play, each player has 50% value when
ties count as half a win.

In phase one, each possible pair should be chosen 1/3 of the time:

- rock + paper
- rock + scissors
- paper + scissors

EXAMPLE

Player 1 reveals rock + paper.
Player 2 reveals rock + scissors.

Optimal phase-two strategy:

- Player 1 keeps rock 2/3 and paper 1/3.
- Player 2 keeps rock 2/3 and scissors 1/3.
- Player 1's value in this matchup is 2/3.

Paper matters because it beats Player 2's rock. If Player 1 always keeps rock,
Player 2 can always keep rock and force a tie. Player 1 can punish that choice
by sometimes keeping paper. Player 2 therefore sometimes keeps scissors to
punish paper.

VERDICT

The game does not collapse into always choosing rock. Optimal play requires
random choices. Individual revealed-pair matchups can favor one player 2/3 to
1/3, but the full game remains fair because the phase-one pair selection is
also mixed.
