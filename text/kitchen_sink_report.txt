Kitchen Sink Rules Report

Status
- This is the authoritative rules specification for Kitchen Sink.
- It records confirmed rules and matchup outcomes only.
- It does not contain Nash values or calculated balance claims.


Core idea
- Kitchen Sink combines the position game from Spacing Game with the meter game from Charge Block Fireball.
- Players choose actions simultaneously unless one player is Punished.
- Each player has 3 HP.
- The first player to win 2 rounds wins the game.


Match and round structure
- Both players begin the game with 0 bars.
- A player can hold at most 3 bars.
- Both players keep their post-action bars between rounds.
- At the start of each new round:
  - Both players return to 3 HP.
  - Position resets to Neutral.
- A round ends immediately when a player reaches 0 HP.
- There are no simultaneous damage trades.
- Resource costs and successful resource gains from the lethal action resolve before bars carry into the next round.
- A resource gain explicitly interrupted by the opposing action does not resolve.


Position
- Neutral: both players are in their own corner.
- Center/Corner: one player has Center and the other player is Cornered.
- "Hold" means the current position does not change.
- "Reset" means position returns to Neutral.
- Position changes still resolve normally after nonlethal damage.
- Position is reset to Neutral at the beginning of every round.


Actions
- Strike.
- Advance.
- Bait.
- Charge, replaced by Super while the player has 3 bars.
- Special, whose identity depends on the player's position:
  - Neutral: Fireball.
  - Center: Powered Strike.
  - Cornered: Reversal.

Bait permanently replaces the old move name Back.


Meter rules
- Charge gives the user 1 bar when it succeeds.
- Charge cannot raise meter above 3 bars.
- A Charge that moves a player from 2 bars to 3 bars remains a Charge for that action.
- Super becomes selectable on that player's next action.
- At 3 bars, Charge is unavailable and is replaced by Super.
- At 3 bars, the player may still choose Special instead of Super.
- Fireball, Powered Strike, and Reversal each cost 1 bar.
- A player with 0 bars cannot choose Special.
- Super costs all 3 bars.
- Every selected Special or Super pays its cost, including when it is blocked, baited, countered, cancelled, or used during a lethal action.


Concrete outcome language
- This report does not use "beats" as a complete rules result.
- Every matchup states its concrete effects on HP, bars, position, or turn flow.
- "Interrupted" means the charging player receives no bar.
- Unless an outcome says otherwise, no HP, bar, position, or turn-flow change occurs.


Punished and free moves
- Center Bait can Punish a Cornered player's Strike or Reversal.
- When Punished:
  - The Cornered player cannot act on the next action.
  - The Center player keeps Center and chooses any currently legal action.
  - The chosen action resolves unopposed.
- Examples of unopposed actions:
  - Strike deals 1 damage.
  - Charge gives 1 bar.
  - Powered Strike costs 1 bar and deals 2 damage.
  - Super costs 3 bars and deals 3 damage.
  - Advance and Bait have no useful effect while the player already has Center.
- If the round survives, normal simultaneous action selection resumes after the free move.

Strategic reading
- Bait is a broad defensive and positioning tool.
- In Neutral, Bait gains Center against Strike and Charge, and blocks Fireball.
- Bait reduces Super from 3 damage to 1 damage.
- Center Bait can create a free move by Punishing Corner Strike or Reversal.
- Center makes ordinary attacks more dangerous and makes Charge easier to interrupt.
- Powered Strike converts 1 bar into a 2-damage threat while preserving Center in most matchups.
- Corner has active escape and counterplay:
  - Bait escapes Center Strike.
  - Advance escapes Center Bait.
  - Reversal damages and resets against Center Strike, Advance, Charge, and Powered Strike.
- Reversal itself can be Punished by Center Bait.
- Super is a 3-damage threat against every action except Bait.
- Super also defeats Reversal.
- A player's current HP and position matter when both players spend Super together.
- Because bars carry between rounds, spending meter to secure the current round competes with saving meter for later rounds.


Requirements for later Nash analysis
- The solver must model simultaneous action selection except during a Punished free move.
- A complete match state must include:
  - Each player's round wins.
  - Each player's current HP.
  - Each player's current bars.
  - Current position: Neutral, player one has Center, or player two has Center.
  - Whether a player is Punished and which player receives the free move.
- Legal actions depend on bars and position.
- At 0 bars, Special is unavailable.
- At 3 bars, Super replaces Charge while Special remains available.
- Terminal round states must apply action costs and successful gains before carrying bars into the next round.
- Terminal game states occur when either player earns a second round win.
- Any future Nash report must be calculated from these rules rather than copied from either source game's results.
