Kitchen Sink Balance Exploration

Status
- Final choice: players start the game at 0 bars.
- Final choice: Fireball deals 1 damage.
- Neutral Strike vs Charge is a soft interrupt:
  - Charge gains no bar.
  - Neither player takes damage.
  - Position remains Neutral.
- Neutral Bait vs Charge:
  - Charge gains 1 bar.
  - Bait gains Center.
- This report explains the tested alternatives and the reasoning behind the final choice.
- Values and mixes below came from approximate Nash solves of all 2,880 legal match states.


Why the opening was sensitive
- Neutral Advance vs Charge and Neutral Bait vs Charge produce the same strategic trade:
  - The Advance or Bait player gains Center.
  - The Charge player gains 1 bar.
- The opening therefore depends on which is worth more:
  - Center with 0 bars.
  - Being Cornered with 1 bar.
- A small change to Fireball damage moved this resulting state across 50% value.
- Crossing that boundary caused a large, discontinuous change in the opening equilibrium.


Version explored: Strike does not interrupt Neutral Charge
- Neutral Strike allowed Charge to gain its bar.
- Opening Nash mix:
  - Strike 1.23%.
  - Advance 39.67%.
  - Charge 59.09%.
- Strike mainly existed to stop Advance from becoming automatic.
- Charge was too safe because Strike could not deny its permanent, round-carrying resource gain.
- This version was rejected.


Version explored: hard Strike interrupt
- Neutral Strike interrupted Charge, denied its bar, and dealt 1 damage.
- With Bait still gaining Center against Charge, the opening became:
  - Strike 31.83%.
  - Advance 31.83%.
  - Bait 36.35%.
  - Charge 0%.
- Equal-resource Neutral states also lost most Charge and Fireball use.
- Strike became healthy, but the punishment was strong enough to suppress the Neutral meter game.
- Removing Bait's Center reward did not solve this because Charge was already outside the equilibrium support.
- This version was rejected.


Version explored: soft Strike interrupt
- Neutral Strike interrupted Charge and denied its bar, but dealt no damage.
- Position remained Neutral.
- With Bait gaining Center against Charge and Fireball dealing 1, the opening became:
  - Strike 31.97%.
  - Advance 31.97%.
  - Bait 36.07%.
  - Charge 0%.
- Charge returned after position or meter developed.
- This produced a useful game structure:
  - Opening footsies establish position.
  - Position creates safer Charge opportunities.
  - Bars introduce Specials and Super.
- This Strike rule was selected.


Version explored: Bait does not gain Center against Charge
- Charge gained 1 bar while Bait left position Neutral.
- With soft Strike interruption, the opening developed many equivalent Strike/Charge equilibria.
- Charge could safely build toward the meter game.
- Strike could stop the bar but caused no damage or position change, creating a repeatable no-progress interaction.
- The solver could return different-looking optimal mixes for the two players because it selected different endpoints from a degenerate equilibrium set.
- Restoring Bait's Center reward made Charge carry a positional risk and produced the preferred opening structure.
- This version was rejected.


Version explored: Fireball deals 2
- Fireball became a serious equal-resource move.
- At Neutral with 1 bar each:
  - Advance 33.77%.
  - Bait 47.38%.
  - Fireball 18.86%.
- However, stronger Fireball increased the value of gaining the first bar.
- Center with 0 bars against Cornered with 1 bar fell to about 49.55% value.
- Because a bar became slightly more valuable than Center, the clean opening equilibrium became Charge vs Charge.
- Strike could stall Charge, but Advance and Bait were slightly losing choices against it.
- Starting at 1 bar would remove the automatic first Charge:
  - Fireball 2 with a 1-bar start produced an Advance/Bait/Fireball opening.
- This version was viable but rejected because it skipped the desired gradual development from spacing into meter play.


Selected version: Fireball 1, start at 0
- With Fireball dealing 1, Center with 0 bars against Cornered with 1 bar is worth about 50.31%.
- Position is therefore slightly more valuable than the first bar.
- Neutral Charge can be challenged by Advance or Bait gaining Center.
- Opening Nash mix:
  - Strike 31.97%.
  - Advance 31.97%.
  - Bait 36.07%.
- At Neutral with 1 bar each:
  - Strike 17.22%.
  - Advance 17.90%.
  - Bait 30.94%.
  - Charge 33.09%.
  - Fireball 0.85%.
- At Neutral with 2 bars against 1 bar, the leading player's mix includes:
  - Advance 20.59%.
  - Bait 9.30%.
  - Charge 38.83%.
  - Fireball 31.29%.


Final reasoning
- The opening is a spacing game rather than an automatic meter-building turn.
- Position determines where Charge is safe and makes the position system structurally important.
- Meter play emerges after the opening instead of replacing it.
- Fireball has a distinct role:
  - It is rare when resources are equal.
  - It becomes a strong conversion tool when a player has a bar lead.
- Powered Strike converts Center and meter into damage.
- Reversal converts meter into escape from Corner.
- Super converts full meter into a round-ending threat.
- Bars carrying between rounds preserve long-term resource decisions.
- This version best combines the two source games while giving the match a gradual increase in complexity.


Known caveat
- Soft Strike vs Charge is a no-progress result.
- Repeating that exact interaction can theoretically stall.
- In the selected opening equilibrium, Charge is unsupported because Advance and Bait can gain favorable position against it.
- The interaction remains worth monitoring during human playtests.
