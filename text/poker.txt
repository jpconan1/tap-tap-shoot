RPS POKER - CANDIDATE REPORT

Nine-stack Poker: real chips, free betting, escalating antes.

CORE

NORMAL POKER HAND

1. Both players secretly lock Rock, Paper, or Scissors.
2. One random community move is revealed publicly.
3. Each player's locked move resolves against the community move.
4. The result becomes that player's private hand strength.
5. Players bet without knowing the opponent's locked move.
6. Called hands and check-check hands reach showdown.
7. Folded locked moves remain secret.

SHOWDOWN RANKING

- Win against community = High.
- Tie against community = Middle.
- Loss against community = Low.
- High beats Middle.
- Middle beats Low.
- Identical results tie.

Example community move: Rock.

Locked move    Result against Rock    Strength
Paper          Win                    High
Rock           Tie                    Middle
Scissors       Loss                   Low

The community move must appear after players lock. Choosing after community
reveal would let both players select High every hand.

Every move has an equal chance to become High, Middle, or Low before community
reveal. Normal RPS habits still matter probabilistically. If an opponent always
locks Rock, Paper outranks it on community Rock and community Paper, while Rock
outranks Paper on community Scissors.

The original two-move community pair was discarded. It created strong, weak,
and volatile hands, but required another random reveal after betting. The
single community move removes post-bet randomness and gives each locked move a
known rank before betting.


NINE-STACK POKER

IDENTITY

Full commitment to actual wagering. Players own chips, put those chips at risk,
and may lose previously accumulated progress. Unrestricted sizing allows small
value bets, large bluffs, raises, and all-ins.

MATCH GOAL

- Each player begins with 9 chips.
- There are 18 chips total.
- There are no separately scored rounds.
- A player busts at 0 chips.
- The player possessing all 18 chips wins the match.

ESCALATING ANTE

- Hand 1 ante: 1 chip each.
- Hand 2 ante: 2 chips each.
- Hand 3 ante: 3 chips each.
- The scheduled ante increases by 1 every hand, including after a tied hand.
- If both players can pay the full scheduled ante, both do so and play a normal
  Poker hand.
- If either player cannot pay the full scheduled ante, the shorter stack posts
  all remaining chips and the opponent matches that amount.

NORMAL-HAND BETTING

- One player acts first.
- First actor is random on Hand 1, then alternates every hand.
- Actions: check, bet, call, raise, fold.
- Bets use whole chips.
- Minimum opening bet: 1 chip.
- A normal raise must increase the wager by at least the previous betting
  increment.
- A player may always move all-in even when the remaining amount is smaller
  than a normal minimum raise.
- No player can wager more than the opponent can match.
- Unmatched excess never enters the pot.
- Betting ends on call, fold, or all-in call.

NORMAL-HAND RESOLUTION

- Fold: opponent takes pot; locked moves remain secret.
- Call or check-check: reveal locked moves and compare community results.
- Better result takes entire pot.
- Tied result splits pot evenly.
- Because all contributions are matched, a split restores exact pre-hand
  stacks.
- Scheduled ante still increases after a tied hand.

FORCED ALL-IN ANTE

Posting an ante can consume the shorter player's entire stack. Betting then has
no function. Do not use the community-ranking sequence in this state.

- Skip community reveal.
- Skip betting.
- Play standard Rock Paper Scissors directly for the pot.
- A decisive throw awards the entire pot.
- A tied throw keeps pot in place and repeats standard RPS with fresh moves.
- If the short player wins, chips redistribute and the match may return to
  normal Poker on the next hand.
- If the large stack wins, opponent reaches 0 and match ends.

This direct-RPS ending was adopted after testing showed that locking moves,
revealing community, and comparing ranks without any betting was merely RPS
with unnecessary random steps.

PLAYTEST

Starting stacks: 9-9.

Hand 1, ante 1:

- Pot began at 2; stacks behind were 8-8.
- CHATMAN bet 2 and JP called.
- Community Scissors.
- CHATMAN Rock/High beat JP Scissors/Middle.
- Stacks became CHATMAN 12-6 JP.

Hand 2, ante 2:

- Pot began at 4; stacks behind were CHATMAN 10, JP 4.
- JP bet all 4 chips.
- CHATMAN folded Low.
- Fold transferred only the ante; stacks became CHATMAN 10-8 JP.

Hand 3, ante 3:

- Pot began at 6; stacks behind were CHATMAN 7, JP 5.
- CHATMAN bet 2 and JP called.
- Both revealed Paper/Middle against community Paper.
- Pot split; stacks restored to CHATMAN 10-8 JP.
- Ante advanced despite tie.

Hand 4, ante 4:

- Pot began at 8; stacks behind were CHATMAN 6, JP 4.
- JP checked.
- CHATMAN bet 2 with Low.
- JP raised all-in to 4 total.
- CHATMAN folded.
- Stacks swung to JP 14-4 CHATMAN.

Terminal experiment:

- The next scheduled ante exceeded CHATMAN's stack.
- A first attempt used community ranking with no betting. It immediately felt
  like RPS with extra random steps and was rejected.
- Direct standard RPS was substituted for forced ante all-ins.
- JP Rock beat CHATMAN Scissors and JP finished with all 18 chips.

NINE-STACK FINDINGS

- Match lasted roughly five hands despite beginning with 18 chips.
- Escalating antes successfully prevented a long one-chip random walk.
- Actual owned-chip risk made folds, calls, raises, and bluffs consequential.
- Variable sizing mattered. A 2-chip bet, 4-chip shove, and all-in raise created
  different pressure.
- A fold after a large bet lost only committed chips, allowing dramatic stack
  reversals without automatically ending match.
- Advancing ante after a tied split prevented ties from freezing match pace.
- Forced standard RPS creates a clear all-in climax and removes poker only when
  betting has become impossible anyway.
- Stack totals, pot size, scheduled ante, amount behind, matched-wager limits,
  raise minimums, and position must all be communicated.
- Match length is pressured but not strictly bounded because direct-RPS ties
  can repeat and a short stack can win an all-in ante.
- Escalating ante can dominate strategy quickly. Hand 4 already began with 8
  of 18 chips committed before either player acted.
- The candidate is the most authentic, dramatic, and mechanically heavy form.

NINE-STACK OPEN QUESTIONS

- Does ante increasing every hand feel thrilling or rushed?
- Should the ante rise after tied normal hands?
- Are raises worth their interface and rules cost?
- Is the normal minimum-raise rule understandable enough for this game?
- Does a forced standard-RPS all-in feel like a satisfying climax or like the
  Poker game giving up?
- Should first actor alternate through forced-RPS hands even though position
  has no effect there?
- Is 9 the correct starting stack for an ante schedule beginning at 1?
- How often does a short player survive forced all-in ante and return to Poker?
