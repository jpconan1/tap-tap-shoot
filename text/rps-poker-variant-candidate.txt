RPS POKER - VARIANT CANDIDATE

STATUS

Strong playtest candidate.

Two players lock RPS moves before a public community move appears. The
community move turns each locked move into a known High, Middle, or Low hand.
Players then check, bet, call, or fold around that private strength.

Won score chips remain permanent. Players increase the value of the current
hand instead of wagering chips they already own.


MATCH GOAL

- The first player to collect 5 score chips wins the match.
- Score chips cannot be lost or wagered.
- A called bet can award 2 chips and may take a player past 5.


HAND SEQUENCE

1. LOCK MOVES

- Each player secretly locks Rock, Paper, or Scissors.
- Locked moves cannot change during the hand.

2. DEAL THE COMMUNITY MOVE

- Rock, Paper, or Scissors is selected uniformly at random.
- The community move is revealed publicly.
- Both players now know their own hand strength, but not the opponent's.

3. BETTING ROUND

- One player acts first.
- The first actor is chosen randomly for the first hand, then alternates every
  hand.
- The first actor may check or bet.
- After a check, the second actor may check or bet.
- After a bet, the opponent must call or fold.
- There are no raises or variable bet sizes.
- A folded locked move remains secret.

4. RESOLUTION AND SCORING

- Check-check: reveal both moves. The better hand gains 1 chip.
- Bet-fold: do not reveal. The bettor gains 1 chip.
- Bet-call: reveal both moves. The better hand gains 2 chips.
- Any tied showdown: both players gain 1 chip.

A bet therefore offers double stakes. Folding concedes the normal 1-chip hand.
Calling accepts the chance to win 2 while risking giving 2 to the bettor.


SHOWDOWN RANKING

Each locked move resolves independently against the community move using
normal RPS rules. Results rank as follows:

- Win beats Tie.
- Tie beats Loss.

Example community move: Rock.

Locked move    Result against Rock    Hand strength
Paper          Win                    High
Rock           Tie                    Middle
Scissors       Loss                   Low

- The move that beats the community is High.
- The move that matches the community is Middle.
- The move that loses to the community is Low.

Every locked move has an equal chance to become High, Middle, or Low before the
community move appears.


WHY BETTING WORKS

- High hands can bet for 2-chip value.
- Middle hands can bet, check, call bluffs, or fold under pressure.
- Low hands can check, fold, or bluff.
- A bet-fold bluff earns the same 1 chip as a normal showdown win.
- Calling with Low cannot beat any hand. Its best possible result is a tie.
- Folded moves remain hidden, so bluffs are not automatically exposed.

The community move must appear after both players lock. If players could choose
after seeing it, both could always select High.


RPS HABITS STILL MATTER

The normal counter cycle survives probabilistically.

If an opponent always locks Rock, Paper beats it on two of the three possible
community moves:

- Community Rock: Paper is High and Rock is Middle.
- Community Paper: Paper is Middle and Rock is Low.
- Community Scissors: Paper is Low and Rock is High.

Habit exploitation is valuable without determining every showdown.


TIES AND MATCH POINT

- Any tied showdown awards 1 chip to each player, so every hand advances the
  match.
- If a tie makes both players reach 5 together, neither wins yet.
- Continue sudden-death hands until one player gains chips without the other.
- During sudden death, a fold or decisive showdown wins the match. Further
  tied showdowns repeat.


MATCH LENGTH

Before simultaneous match point, every hand distributes at least 1 total chip.
A match therefore reaches a winner or sudden death after no more than 9 hands.
Repeated sudden-death ties can extend that bound.

A match may end in 3 hands through called 2-chip wins. Most hands require two
player decisions: one locked move plus one betting action.


IMPARTIAL DEALER

Prototype script:

scripts/rps-poker-dealer.js

Deal one uniformly random community move:

node scripts/rps-poker-dealer.js

The script uses Node's cryptographic random-number generator.


PLAYTEST 1: COIN-STACK VERSION

The earlier version used 3 coins per round, a 1-coin ante, fixed 1-coin bets, a
5-coin round threshold, and first-to-2 round scoring.

- The official match ended 2-0 after 3 hands.
- An extra round lasted 9 hands.
- Coin states could reverse repeatedly between 4-2 and 2-4.
- Betting position strongly constrained decisions at 4-2.
- Four identical-move ties occurred in the 9-hand extra round.
- The system required stacks, antes, pots, round wins, match score, a special
  busted threshold, and split-pot handling.

Verdict: discard the coin-stack economy. It creates variable length and rules
weight around the betting mechanism.


PLAYTEST 2: SCORE-CHIP VERSION

Result: CHATMAN defeated JP 6-1 after 5 hands.

- Every hand contained a bet.
- Three bets ended in folds, each awarding 1 chip.
- One called bet tied and awarded 1 chip to each player.
- One called bet was decisive and awarded 2 chips to the winner.
- No move was revealed after a fold.
- The score advanced every hand.
- Low facing a bet had no winning call outcome; folding was correct.
- At 1-4, the trailing player bet because a passive loss would also end the
  match, while a 2-chip win could begin a comeback.
- The complete match required 10 locked-move decisions and 11 betting actions
  across both players.


PLAYTEST QUESTIONS

- Is betting frequent because it is interesting, or because checking is too
  weak?
- Are 1-chip folds and 2-chip called wins the right rewards?
- Does awarding 1 chip each on a tied showdown feel natural?
- Does the score-chip presentation support the poker fantasy despite players
  never risking chips they already own?
- Is first to 5 consistently the right match length?
- Does first position have a meaningful advantage or disadvantage?
- Does the community move feel like fair uncertainty or an arbitrary reversal
  of the players' underlying RPS matchup?
- What player-facing terms should replace community move, High, Middle, Low,
  bet, and call?


CURRENT VERDICT

Score-chip RPS Poker preserves bluffing, calling, folds, hidden information,
and double-stakes tension without a reversible coin economy. It fits the
existing permanent-score presentation and guarantees progress every hand. The
main unresolved question is whether betting choices remain rich once players
understand that Low cannot win a called showdown.
